The **Translational Motor Neurophysiology Lab** is led by Dr Filipe Nascimento at University College London (UCL). Our work focuses on the spinal microcircuits that control movement — motoneurons and the premotor interneurons that shape their output — and on how these circuits adapt, compensate and eventually fail in conditions such as amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), also known as motor neuron disease (MND).

In ALS, clinical weakness only appears after more than half of the motoneurons supplying a muscle have been lost, which reflects a remarkable capacity of the nervous system to compensate. We want to understand these compensations: which spinal circuits adapt, when, and how, so that windows of neuronal resilience can be used for earlier diagnosis, better prognosis and new therapies.

To do this, we combine _in vitro_, _ex vivo_ and _in vivo_ electrophysiology in mice with **high-density surface electromyography (HDsEMG)** and motor unit decoding in humans. Because the neuromuscular system is so similar between species, we can ask the same questions in mice and in people.
